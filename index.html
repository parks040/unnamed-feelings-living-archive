Unnamed FeelingsLiving Archive

Not every feeling has a name.

A single feeling is rarely just one thing. It often holds several emotions tangled together, too complex to be defined by one word, one image, or one symbol. I believe there is beauty hidden in the smallest emotions we often overlook. Through drawing, collecting, and experimenting, I explore these feelings, hoping to encourage others to look more closely at their own emotional experiences.

01 / Sketching

Sketchbook · 01:29

I began drawing to explore emotions that words could not fully describe. Rather than defining what I felt, I allowed colors, textures, and abstract forms to emerge intuitively.

02 / The Limitation

At first, I tried to express emotions by assigning each drawing a specific label. However, I realized that naming a feeling before drawing it limited my interpretation.

Instead of defining what I felt, I began recording the moments that made me feel something.

03 / Repetition & Difference

As I collected more drawings, I noticed that certain words and sentences appeared repeatedly, yet the images associated with them were completely different.

I realized that even when we use the same words to describe our feelings, each experience is shaped by a different moment, environment, and state of mind.

The words may repeat, but the feelings never exist in exactly the same way.

04 / Digital Reinterpretation

Digital · 00:18

I reinterpreted each hand-drawn sketch digitally, not to improve it, but to explore how the same emotional moment could take another form through color, transparency, layering, and texture.

05 / The Book

Book · 256 images

I brought the original sketches and their digital reinterpretations together in a book, placing the hand-drawn version on the left and its digital counterpart on the right. The final pages serve as an index, documenting each drawing’s original sentence, date, and page number.

06 / The Anatomy of Unnamed Feelings ↗︎

I built a digital archive, The Anatomy of Unnamed Feelings, to turn the book’s index into something searchable. Each drawing is recorded with its original sentence, date, and visual qualities, so it can be compared with others that share the same sentence or word.

09 / ASCII

Drawings in characters

I translated my hand-drawn sketches into ASCII, rebuilding each form from typed characters. I wanted to see what would remain of each feeling once its color and texture were removed and only characters were left.

10 / Emotional Receipts

Receipts record what I buy, then are often thrown away. I wanted to use them to document what I feel instead, creating one receipt for each day.

By placing my personal emotions within a standardized format, I highlight the contrast between what can be measured and what cannot. There are no prices, because feelings cannot be bought or exchanged.

Process · ASCII conversion

To print them on a thermal receipt printer, which prints only text, I first converted each drawing into ASCII.
